How to Find Roofing Jobs: 7 Proven Ways Contractors Get Consistent Work

By Roof Scout · 8 min read

TL;DR: The fastest way to find roofing jobs in 2026? Scan your ZIP with Roof Scout — AI finds roofs showing wear, you get exclusive leads for $1–$2 each. Free trial, no credit card.

Every roofing contractor knows the feast-or-famine cycle. One month you're turning down work, the next you're wondering where the phone went. The contractors who stay busy year-round aren't luckier — they just have systems for finding work instead of waiting for it.

Here are seven proven methods, from old-school to cutting-edge.

1. Door-to-door canvassing (still works)

Knocking on doors after a hailstorm is the oldest trick in the book — because it works. But blind canvassing burns hours. The smart version: canvass neighborhoods where you already know roofs are aging. Drive the streets, look for curling shingles, missing tabs, and moss. Targeted canvassing converts 3–5x better than random knocking.

2. Storm chasing (high reward, high effort)

Following hail and wind events can fill a pipeline fast. The downside: you're competing with every other storm chaser who showed up, travel costs eat margin, and you're at the mercy of weather. It works, but it's not a system you control.

3. Referrals and repeat customers

The cheapest leads you'll ever get. A simple follow-up program — a thank-you text after the job, a check-in at the one-year mark — generates referrals on autopilot. Most contractors leave this money on the table by never asking.

4. Google Business Profile and local SEO

When a homeowner's roof leaks, they Google "roofer near me." If you're not in the top three map results, you're invisible. Claim your profile, collect reviews aggressively, and post updates weekly. This is slow to build but compounds over time.

5. Lead marketplaces (know the math)

HomeAdvisor, Angi, and Thumbtack will sell you leads — at $40 to $110 each, shared with 4–5 competitors. Do the math before you buy: if you close 1 in 5 shared leads at $75 each, your customer acquisition cost is $375. Sometimes worth it, often not. Shared leads are a race to the bottom on price.

6. Direct mail to targeted neighborhoods

Old-fashioned, but targeted mail still pulls. The key word is targeted — blanketing a ZIP code is expensive. Mailing 200 homeowners whose roofs actually show wear is cheap. The trick is knowing which 200.

7. AI-powered prospecting (the new edge)

This is where the industry is heading. Instead of driving around looking for worn roofs, software analyzes aerial imagery across entire ZIP codes, flags properties showing visible signs of wear, and hands you a prioritized list with addresses and evidence.

You still knock on the door — but now you're knocking on the right doors. Contractors using this approach report spending less time prospecting and more time closing, because every conversation starts with "I noticed your roof…" backed by actual imagery.
